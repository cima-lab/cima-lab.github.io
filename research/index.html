01 · Funded work

Research grants

Both awards sit in the health and life sciences track of the Dubai RDI Grant Programme, delivered through Dubai Future Foundation, and both are led from Canadian University Dubai.

vEM
Dubai RDI Grant Programme Health and life sciences Active

Towards high-resolution 3D reconstruction in volume electron microscopy

Volume electron microscopy resolves cells and tissue in three dimensions at nanometre scale, but the raw output is thousands of imperfect serial sections that have to be repaired before anything can be measured. The grant builds an AI processing chain for that data: high-precision stitching frameworks that handle image transformations, deep learning alignment that copes with slice damage, self-supervised algorithms for 3D reconstruction, and large pre-trained models for automated segmentation. The objective is to cut noise and raise effective resolution so that volume EM analysis becomes routine rather than specialist work, and so the tooling reaches groups that could not build it themselves.

Principal investigator: Dr. Haythem El-Messiry Partners: Shandong University, MBZUAI Funder: Dubai RDI Grant Programme, Dubai Future Foundation Team: Tahamid Hossain, graduate research assistant Previously: Dr. Hanan Hussein, postdoctoral researcher

Dementia
Dubai RDI Grant Programme Health and life sciences Active

Identifying prodromal and preclinical markers for predicting diagnosis and progression in dementia

The project builds an explainable AI system for detecting dementia before it becomes clinically obvious, with a focus on prodromal Alzheimer's disease. Cognitive, neuropsychiatric and biomarker data are combined so the reasoning behind a prediction remains clear to a clinician instead of collapsing into a single opaque score. The deliverable is a diagnostic software platform that pairs machine learning with rule-based methods and is reachable as a cloud service, developed alongside training for local health-technology staff.

Principal investigator: Dr. Firuz Kamalov Lead institution: Canadian University Dubai Data: Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) Team: Junaid Aziz, graduate research assistant; Anthony Ibrahim, undergraduate research assistant Affiliated faculty: Dr. Mohammad Nami

02 · Under the imaging grant

Key projects

Four codebases in the lab's GitHub organisation form a single pipeline. A raw acquisition is stitched into coherent sections, damaged regions are repaired from their neighbours, the axial resolution is restored so the volume is isotropic, and only then is the volume segmented. Each stage runs on its own and each has a public repository.

github.com/cima-lab
  1. Stage 1StitchingTiles from one section are aligned into a single seamless image. vEMstitch.
  2. Stage 2InpaintingTorn, folded or missing regions are filled from the sections above and below. FlowInpaint.
  3. Stage 3Isotropic reconstructionThe coarse axial direction is restored so that voxels become cubic. vEMINR.
  4. Stage 4SegmentationOrganelles are traced as instances through the volume. Microsam_llrd.
Stage 4

Microsam_llrd

A PyTorch framework for fine-tuning MicroSAM on electron microscopy with LoRA and layer-wise learning-rate decay. Low-rank adapters are inserted from encoder block 6 at rank 8 with a layer decay of 0.95, which keeps the trainable parameter count small while still adapting the segmentation backbone to EM texture. The repository includes dataset loaders for Lucchi, UroCell, Kasthuri and VNC, augmentation-based upsampling for class imbalance, an optimizer configuration built for LoRA with LLRD, and entry points for training, checkpoint merging, inference and evaluation of generalist and specialist mitochondria instance segmentation.

Stage 3

vEMINR

Ultra-fast isotropic reconstruction for volume electron microscopy using implicit neural representations. Rather than assuming a fixed downsampling model, the method learns the true degradation pattern of the low-resolution axis and represents the volume as a continuous function, which restores axial detail at a fraction of the usual cost. It was published in Advanced Science in 2026 and tested across eight public datasets, with the EPFL electron microscopy data used as the reference benchmark.

Stage 1

vEMstitch

Fully automatic stitching of serial-section electron microscopy tiles, robust to rotation and to the local non-rigid distortion introduced during sectioning. The workflow runs a fast, robust basic stitch first, then an optional refinement pass that re-extracts features only for the sections where misalignment is still visible. Python and C++ implementations are provided, validated on simulated data derived from CREMI covering translation, rotation and rigid transforms with local distortion at several noise levels, as well as on real mussel tissue imagery.

Stage 2

FlowInpaint

An image inpainting algorithm for serial sectioning images, guided by optical flow from neighbour images. Damaged or missing regions are recovered in three passes: an initial interpolated reference, a flow-refined reference, and a guided inpainting step that fuses the two. Filling a section from its neighbours rather than from the section alone keeps the repaired area consistent through the depth of the volume, which matters because every later stage assumes continuity between slices.

Related work: 3D registration between stitching and reconstruction

Alignment across sections decides whether a stitched stack can be reconstructed at all. Joint work with Shandong University treats it as trajectory optimisation on a manifold, using a dual objective that balances global trajectory smoothness against local structure, so ultrastructure is preserved and genuine tissue deformation is not flattened away.

Zhenbang Zhang, Jingtong Feng, Hongjia Li, Haythem El-Messiry, Zhiqiang Xu, Renmin Han. "Unsupervised trajectory optimization for 3D registration in serial section electron microscopy using neural ODEs." Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 2026.

From Volumetrics to 3D Tensors: A Multi-Cohort Evaluation of Machine Learning and Deep Learning for Alzheimer's Classification

A multi-cohort comparison of classical machine learning on volumetric and voxel-based morphometry features against 3D convolutional networks trained end to end on raw MRI tensors. Models are evaluated on ADNI and OASIS with internal cross-validation and zero-shot cross-cohort testing, so predictive performance, resilience to domain shift and clinical sensitivity are measured on the same footing.

Adaptive cascading artificial intelligence for Alzheimer's disease assessment: a clinically oriented narrative review and implementation framework

A narrative review and implementation framework for cascading AI in Alzheimer's assessment, built around progressive screening, biomarker-guided protocols using blood-based markers, and longitudinal monitoring. The argument concerns deployment rather than benchmarks: why accuracy reported on curated cohorts does not carry over to a working clinical pathway, and what an implementable pathway would look like instead.

RDoC-informed explainable AI as a paradigm for multilevel Alzheimer's disease diagnosis and progression prediction: a systematic review

A systematic review of 56 studies proposing that explainable AI outputs be mapped onto the Research Domain Criteria framework, so biomarkers from different organisational levels are restructured into cognitively meaningful domains. Read that way, model behaviour gains interpretability and mechanistic validity and can support a clinical decision, rather than arriving as a set of isolated predictions.

Path-sampled integrated gradients

A feature-attribution method that takes expected values over sampled baselines along the interpolation path instead of committing to a single reference point. The paper reports faster convergence and reduced variance while keeping the axiomatic properties attribution methods for explainable AI are expected to satisfy.
