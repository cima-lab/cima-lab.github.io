SEAST hosts a workshop on AI in medical imaging and diagnostic applications

Researchers from Canadian University Dubai and Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence met at the CUD Incubator on 14 February 2026 to present work on generative modelling, explainable AI and 3D medical image segmentation.

18 February 2026Canadian University Dubai, City WalkSource: CUD News

The School of Engineering, Applied Science and Technology (SEAST) at Canadian University Dubai hosted the workshop AI in Medical Imaging and Diagnostic Applications on Saturday, 14 February 2026, at the CUD Incubator. Participants included researchers from Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence and students from the CUD Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence programme.

The programme covered three areas at the centre of CIMA Lab research. Zhenbang Zhang presented a motion-residual, conflict-aware time reversal framework for generative inbetweening, with an emphasis on temporal consistency in sequential imaging data. Rohan Mitra gave a practical session on explainable AI, covering attribution techniques, the limits of interpretability and what it means to deploy such systems in high-stakes diagnostic settings. Dr. Hanan Hussein, then a postdoctoral researcher with the lab, presented advances in interactive deep segmentation for 3D medical volumes, including hybrid convolutional and attention architectures and user-in-the-loop refinement for precise anatomical delineation.

The work presented comes from the two research awards that the Dubai Future Foundation granted to Canadian University Dubai, the same awards that fund the CIMA Lab programmes on 3D reconstruction in volume electron microscopy and early markers of dementia. The discussion after the talks focused on how medical AI methods move from benchmark results into clinical use.
